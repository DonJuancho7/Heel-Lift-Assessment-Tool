About This Tool

This clinical decision support tool guides chiropractic interns and clinicians through the complete heel lift utilization assessment — from radiographic MD/AD calculation through the six-point decision matrix and dosing principles. Designed for use at Keagan Devoe (Student Intern).

MD vs. AD — Core Definitions
Measured Difference (MD)

The raw femur head height difference visible on the AP pelvic radiograph. This is the apparent leg length discrepancy before accounting for pelvic subluxation mechanics — it may be inflated or deflated depending on listing type and side.

Actual Difference (AD)

The true femur height difference after correcting for pelvic subluxation. This is the portion of the leg length discrepancy that chiropractic adjustment alone cannot resolve — the genuine structural anatomical deficit. The AD is calculated by determining how much pelvic listings artificially alter femur head height and removing that contribution from the MD.

AD = MD ± (Listing Sum × 0.4)
Every 5mm of listing correction = 2mm femur height change
Multiplier: 0.4 (always)
Round correction to nearest whole mm
Pelvic Listing Mechanics
Why PI is married to EX

The SI joint does not rotate like a simple hinge — it has an oblique axis running through the auricular surface (the pivot). When the ilium rotates posteriorly around this pivot, the PSIS swings back and down (PI) while the ASIS simultaneously swings forward and up (EX). These motions are mechanically coupled through the auricular surface and cannot occur independently. The auricular surface has an interlocking topography that permits rotation but prevents pure translational sliding.

Effect on Femur Head Height
  • PI/Ex listings artificially lower the femur head. Correcting them recovers lost height (raises the femur).
  • AS/In listings artificially raise the femur head. Correcting them removes artificial height (lowers the femur).
Married vs. Unmarried Listings
  • Married PI+EX or AS+IN — same auricular surface direction → ADD subscripts
  • Unmarried PI+IN or AS+EX — opposing forces → SUBTRACT subscripts
Same Side vs. Opposite Side Listings
  • Listing on SAME side as short leg, PI/Ex: lowers the already-low femur → correcting raises it → subtract from MD → AD decreases.
  • Listing on SAME side as short leg, AS/In: raises the already-low femur → correcting lowers it → add to MD → AD increases.
  • Listing on OPPOSITE side (high side), PI/Ex: lowers the high femur → correcting raises the high side → gap widens → add to MD → AD increases.
  • Listing on OPPOSITE side (high side), AS/In: raises the high femur → correcting lowers the high side → gap narrows → subtract from MD → AD decreases.
  • If the result is negative, the short leg flips to the other side. Take the absolute value.
Utilization Threshold

Heel lift management is only considered when the AD > 6mm (or ≥ 7mm). An AD at or below 6mm does not warrant a heel lift — chiropractic adjustment alone is sufficient to manage the functional component.

The AD threshold is a necessary but not sufficient condition. All six utilization criteria must be satisfied before proceeding with lift implementation.

Six-Point Utilization Decision Matrix
1 & 2. X-ray Analysis & Threshold (Auto)

If the MD is confirmed radiographically, the AD is calculated correctly using the 0.4 multiplier, and AD exceeds 6mm — these first two checkpoints pass automatically. If AD ≤ 6mm, the assessment ends here.

3. Scoliosis Check

The scoliosis must be rotatory (spinous processes rotate toward the concavity), a single major curve, with the convexity on the side of the short leg.

  • Utilize Rotatory, single major curve, convex toward short leg, spinous processes to concavity
  • Contraindicated Simple (non-rotatory) scoliosis
  • Contraindicated Double major curves
  • Contraindicated No lumbar curve present
4. Sitting Test

The lumbar curve must disappear when the patient sits. Sitting removes ground-reaction forces and lower extremity influence on the pelvis. Disappears = functional/AO-driven. Persists = idiopathic scoliosis — do not utilize.

5. Sacral Inferiority (Auto)

The sacrum must be inferior on the same side as the short leg. This is auto-populated from your Step 1 entries. If sacrum is level or inferior on the opposite side — do not utilize.

6. History — Failed Conservative Stabilization

The patient must have already failed conservative SI stabilization: gluteus maximus strengthening exercises were ineffective, and chiropractic adjustments correct the subluxation but it returns within days.

Scoring: 5 or 6 of 6 criteria = qualify. 4 or fewer = do not qualify.

Dosing Principles
Start Small

Always begin with the minimum lift amount needed to meet the clinical goal. Compensation is not 1:1 — a 2mm lift may only change sacral inferiority by 1mm.

Two Absolute Ceilings
  • Ceiling 1: Never exceed the AD value.
  • Ceiling 2: Never exceed the amount needed to level the sacral inferiority.
Full-Time Compliance

Patient must wear the lift during ALL weight-bearing hours. Partial wear creates compensatory stress on the SI joint and spine.

Re-Imaging Protocol

Re-image at 1 month. Patient must be wearing the lift at time of imaging. Purpose: verify SI joint stabilization and/or lateral curve reduction.

Terminology

We manage the deficit — not "treat" with the lift. The lift compensates for a structural reality that adjustment cannot resolve. This distinction is clinically and legally important.

Measurement Methods
  • High Accuracy Plain film radiography — gold standard for MD/AD. Most accurate and consistent.
  • Do Not Use Supine tape measurement — wildly inaccurate and inconsistent. Never use for MD/AD calculation.
  • Diagnostic Sitting examination — differentiates AO-driven curves from idiopathic scoliosis. Not used for measurement.
Hyper-Pronation Note

Correcting hyper-pronation does NOT affect the MD/AD calculation. It pulls the ilia anteriorly, shifting the acetabulum posterior/cephalad. This distortion is already factored out in the MD/AD math.

SI Joint Stabilization Protocol

Heel lifts for SI joint stabilization are only indicated after conservative care has failed. Prerequisites before implementing a lift:

  • Gluteus maximus strengthening exercises tried and found ineffective
  • Chiropractic adjustments correct the subluxation but it returns within days

We manage the deficit. We do not use lifts as therapy.
